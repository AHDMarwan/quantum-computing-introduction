# Photonic Quantum Computing

## 1. Information carried by light

Photonic quantum computing uses photons or optical modes as carriers of quantum information. Information can be encoded in polarization, path, time bins, frequency bins, photon number, or continuous quadratures.

Therefore photonic computing includes both discrete-variable and continuous-variable approaches.

## 2. Optical operations

Linear-optical components include

- beam splitters,
- phase shifters,
- interferometers,
- squeezers for CV systems,
- single-photon or homodyne detectors,
- and active feed-forward.

Photons interact weakly, so deterministic two-qubit gates are nontrivial. Measurement-induced nonlinearities, ancillary photons, cluster states, and matter-photon interfaces are important strategies.

## 3. Strengths

Photonics offers

- low decoherence during propagation,
- room-temperature transmission,
- natural networking capability,
- and compatibility with integrated optical fabrication.

## 4. Challenges

Major challenges include

- efficient deterministic sources,
- low-loss routing,
- high-efficiency detection,
- indistinguishability,
- large-scale switching and multiplexing,
- and the overhead of probabilistic entangling operations in some architectures.

## 5. Measurement-based photonics

Photonic systems are natural candidates for measurement-based computation: large entangled cluster states can be generated and consumed by adaptive measurements. This is a concrete example of a hardware platform aligning naturally with a computational model different from conventional gate sequencing.
