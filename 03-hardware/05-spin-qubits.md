# Semiconductor and Spin Qubits

## 1. Spin as a qubit

Spin qubits encode information in electronic or nuclear spin degrees of freedom, often in semiconductor quantum dots, donor atoms, or related nanostructures.

A simple electron-spin encoding is

\[
|0\rangle\equiv|\downarrow\rangle,
\qquad
|1\rangle\equiv|\uparrow\rangle.
\]

## 2. Control

Single-spin rotations can be driven by oscillating magnetic or electric fields. Two-qubit gates often exploit exchange interactions between nearby electrons. Architectures differ substantially in material system and encoding, including single-spin, singlet-triplet, exchange-only, and donor-based qubits.

## 3. Strengths

Potential strengths include

- very small physical footprint,
- compatibility with semiconductor fabrication,
- long coherence in carefully engineered materials,
- and prospects for dense integration.

## 4. Challenges

Challenges include

- device variability,
- charge and magnetic noise,
- high-fidelity readout and control,
- cryogenic classical electronics,
- long-range coupling,
- and routing/connectivity at scale.

## 5. Original proposal

The Loss–DiVincenzo proposal established a foundational architecture for quantum computation using electron spins in quantum dots and exchange-based two-qubit gates.
