# Adiabatic Quantum Computing

## 1. Basic idea

Adiabatic quantum computing encodes the solution of a computational problem in the ground state of a final Hamiltonian. One prepares the ground state of an easier Hamiltonian $H_0$ and evolves under

```math
H(s)=(1-s)H_0+sH_P,
\qquad s\in[0,1].
```

The schedule $s=s(t)$ controls the interpolation.

## 2. Adiabatic condition

Informally, if the evolution is sufficiently slow compared with inverse powers of the relevant spectral gap, transitions out of the target instantaneous eigenspace can be suppressed. The exact conditions depend on the chosen adiabatic theorem, smoothness assumptions, schedule, and gap structure.

When the instantaneous ground state is nondegenerate, a common gap definition is

```math
\Delta(s)=E_1(s)-E_0(s),
```

with

```math
\Delta_{\min}=\min_s\Delta(s).
```

If the ground space is degenerate, the relevant gap is instead the spectral separation between that ground-state subspace and the rest of the spectrum. The simple expression $E_1-E_0$ therefore assumes a nondegenerate ground state.

## 3. Computational equivalence

Aharonov et al. established polynomial equivalence between the standard adiabatic model and the conventional circuit model. Thus adiabatic quantum computing is not merely a heuristic optimizer; in its universal formulation it is a general model of quantum computation.

## 4. Relation to optimization

Many presentations choose $H_P$ so that its ground state represents an optimal solution to a classical cost function. This makes adiabatic computation look similar to quantum annealing. The distinction is that universal AQC is a computational model, whereas quantum annealing is commonly used as a more specialized optimization paradigm and may operate outside an ideal closed-system adiabatic limit.

## 5. Relation to QAOA

QAOA alternates evolutions generated by a cost Hamiltonian and a mixer Hamiltonian:

```math
\prod_{j=1}^p e^{-i\beta_jH_M}e^{-i\gamma_jH_C}.
```

This resembles a discretized, variationally optimized Hamiltonian path, but QAOA is a gate-based variational algorithm and should not be identified with adiabatic computation.
